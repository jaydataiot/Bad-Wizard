The Watson Manor Investigations

Grab-a-Ghost

“Some houses are haunted. Watson Manor is hungry.”

A cooperative, scenario-driven survival-horror investigation. Enter a living manor, test contradictory clues, learn what Gwendolyn and Gracie are hiding, and escape before the house rewrites you.

2–6 Players60–120 MinutesAges 18+Narrative Survival Horror

Watson Manor Is Waiting

No one remembers exactly when Watson Manor died. Above Black Briar Hill, Gwendolyn Watson and her granddaughter Gracie still tend dead flowers, dine on century-old meals, and repeat a daily routine that should have ended generations ago. Then a preacher entered during a violent storm and never returned. Since that night, rooms have moved, portraits have whispered, and visitors have disappeared.

Your team arrives expecting an exaggerated legend. Instead, you find a house that remembers intrusions—and has been waiting for witnesses.

The Truth Changes Every Game

A face-down Truth card secretly defines what happened in the manor. It determines the real antagonist, which clues can be trusted, how the Watsons behave, which rooms may appear, and which endings are possible.

Your Objective

Gather credible Evidence, identify the hidden Truth, execute a legal Final Plan, and survive the Revelation. There is no conventional winner and no point score—only a collective ending and an individual fate for every investigator.

Investigator Traits

  • Health for physical survival
  • Composure for mental control
  • Insight to interpret clues and rituals
  • Resolve to resist possession
  • Presence to negotiate and comfort
  • Corruption for dangerous supernatural power

Every Round

The House Stirs

Resolve persistent rooms, hauntings, possessions, and the consequences of last round’s Noise.

Investigator Turns

Beginning with the Lead Investigator, each player takes up to three actions.

The Manor Responds

Draw a Haunting. At Threat 5 or higher, draw two and suffer the worse result.

Gwendolyn & Gracie

Resolve their behavior cards, modified by Trust, Wrath, and the secret Truth.

Time Passes

Advance Time and unlock stronger manifestations, new decks, or the Revelation.

Actions & Tests

Move, explore, search, investigate, interact, use equipment, barricade doors, perform rituals, assist, rest, or escape. Tests roll a number of six-sided dice equal to the relevant trait; every 5 or 6 is a success.

  • 0: catastrophic failure
  • 1: partial success with a cost
  • 2: full success
  • 3+: critical success and a bonus

Evidence, Not Answers

Clues provide names, dates, testimony, symbols, and ritual details, but they must be verified before becoming Evidence. Doubt can invalidate suspicious leads, contradictions demand comparison, and convincing Red Herrings may trigger the wrong ritual.

When the Truth is revealed, some apparently reliable clues may turn against the investigation.

The Living Manor

  • Room Shift: tiles rotate, swap, disappear, or become something else.
  • House Hunger: Noise, death, broken taboos, and delay make the manor stronger.
  • Impossible Doors: a threshold may lead somewhere different depending on who opens it.
  • The Rot: corrupted rooms eventually collapse or become part of the house’s body.
  • Watching Portraits: the manor records actions to expose or punish during Revelation.
  • Memory Marks: campaign investigations leave scars the house remembers.

Gwendolyn Watson

She may be a jailer, victim, monster, guardian, or vessel. Her Wrath rises when the team breaks household rules, enters forbidden rooms, damages the manor, or threatens Gracie. At high Wrath, she locks rooms, confiscates relics, summons spirits, and forces investigators into the daily routine.

Gracie Watson

Gracie communicates through drawings, nursery rhymes, games, altered poems, and contradictory memories. Earn her Trust by protecting her and listening without force. Betray it and she may misdirect the party—or unleash a manifestation.

Ghosts Have Motives

Every Ghost has a Nature, Need, Trigger, Weakness, and Resolution. Spirits may be hostile, protective, confused, bound, deceptive, or merely an Echo of a repeated event. They are not captured for points; they must be escaped, appeased, questioned, released, bound, or exorcised.

The Daily Routine

Garden, Breakfast, Poetry, Housework, Supper, Bedtime. Comply to reduce Wrath, interrupt to uncover memories, imitate the family to access hidden rooms, or sabotage the pattern at the risk of an immediate Manifestation.

The Revelation

When Time expires, House Hunger reaches 10, enough Evidence is verified, a Truth trigger is met, or a major ritual is performed, reveal what Watson Manor has been hiding. Invalid clues collapse, Red Herrings become consequences, and the survivors must choose a Final Plan they can actually support.

Escape with ProofLeave credible evidence behind you.
Exorcise or ReleaseCorrectly identify what must be banished—or freed.
Seal the ManorTrap the evil, even if someone remains inside.
Burn It DownDestroy the house, the proof, and perhaps its prisoners.
Break the LoopReconstruct and alter the Watsons’ final day.
Accept, or Feed, the HousePurchase survival at an unforgivable price.

Every Fate Is Personal

Investigators may earn a Clean Escape, return damaged or discredited, become Claimed or Missing, die inside, or carry something worse into the outside world. Dead players remain involved through spirit actions and control of certain hauntings.

Modes of Play

  • Standard Investigation: full cooperative mystery
  • Unreliable Witness: private, possibly false memories
  • Secret Bargain: one investigator has a private escape condition
  • The House Remembers: persistent campaign consequences
  • After Midnight: the harshest cards and irreversible outcomes
  • Merciful Haunting: reduced graphic content and lethality

Content Guidance

Grab-a-Ghost is intended for adults. It may include psychological and physical abuse, child endangerment, death, grief, possession, occult rituals, body horror, confinement, domestic cruelty, implied violence, suicide references, religious horror, and betrayal. A card-content index allows groups to remove specific themes; the After Midnight deck is always optional.
